# 3-minute pitch — Predictive Airport Capacity & Delay-Risk

**0:00 — User & status quo (30s)**
Meet Dana, airline ops duty manager at ATL. Today she finds out about a capacity squeeze when ATC cuts the arrival rate.
By then aircraft are holding, taxi queues build and crews time out. Every decision is reactive.
She needs 3–12 hours of warning, and a reason she can trust.

**0:30 — What the tool shows (30s)**
Pick an airport, a time and a 3/6/12-hour horizon. You get:
- predicted capacity stress per 3-hour block, against that airport's normal band;
- a NORMAL / WATCH / ACTION card;
- the weather timeline behind it.
The numbers come from ML and statistics; the recommendation comes from plain rules.
Every card lists the exact METAR values and timestamps it used. Nothing in the numbers is generated by an LLM.

**1:00 — Live demo (60s)** *(use a real 2025 storm day once real outputs exist; synthetic default is ATL 2025-05-20)*
1. Set ATL, 10:00 local, 6h. The stress bar for the afternoon block turns orange/red above p80/p90. The card says "consider reducing arrival rate / GDP for 14:00–17:00".
2. Open the evidence: predicted 52/100 flights vs p90 31/100, issued 08:00Z.
3. Point to a crosswind marker flagged *upper bound*: direction unknown, so the tool says "verify on ATIS" instead of acting.
4. Open the At-risk flights tab: flagged flights with their top factors. Then log the decision ("Accept, note: GDP requested"). The human decides.

**2:00 — Honest results (30s)**
Test period is 2025, never trained on. Base rate of >45-min delays is 13%.
- Calendar-only baseline: AUC 0.645.
- With weather: AUC ≈ 0.68, PR-AUC 0.22 → 0.25. That is a modest but real lift.
- Calibration is shown, and so is transfer from ATL to DFW.
ATL and DFW are compared only as rates per 100 flights.

**2:30 — One failure, limits, next (30s)**
Failure: DFW, an afternoon thunderstorm line was missed at the 12h horizon. Our inputs are observations, and the storm formed after issue time.
Limits:
- the data is a sample, so stress is a proxy, not true AAR;
- the weather timestamps had a time-zone bug, which we fixed;
- wind direction comes from public METARs, with an upper-bound fallback.
Next: TAF/convective forecasts, the full schedule for true AAR, and a pilot with one hub ops desk.
